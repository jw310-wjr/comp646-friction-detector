%%%%%%%%% ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
Effective teaching requires noticing student confusion and adjusting
instruction in real time.  We present a system that automatically detects
\emph{pedagogical friction}: moments where a low-quality instructional
strategy coincides with elevated student confusion.  Our pipeline fuses
per-frame facial affect (DeepFace), transcribed speech (Whisper), and
strategy labels from an ELECTRA Talk Moves classifier or keyword heuristic
on a 30-second temporal grid, then applies a large language model (Claude)
as a second-pass precision filter.  We validate at three scales: a full
multimodal run on 10\,min of TIMSS AU1 video (DeepFace + Whisper + LLM),
a transcript-only analysis across 53 TIMSS lessons spanning 7 countries
(5\,113 bins), and a human-annotated ground-truth evaluation on the full
AU1 lesson (87 bins).  The two-stage pipeline achieves F1=1.00 against
human annotation, versus F1=0.10 for the heuristic alone and F1=0.00 for
ELECTRA alone, demonstrating that LLM reranking is essential to reach
human-level precision in friction detection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

High-quality teaching requires moment-by-moment diagnosis of student
understanding and strategy adjustment~\cite{sherinvanes2009}.  Tools such
as Tutor CoPilot~\cite{tutorcopilot} and Edu-ConvoKit~\cite{educonvokit}
offer transcript-based feedback, but no existing system jointly models
instructional strategy quality \emph{and} student affect to surface
actionable friction moments.  D'Mello and Graesser~\cite{dmello2012}
established that confusion, when unresolved, impedes deep learning,
motivating our use of facial affect as a confusion proxy.
Talk Moves~\cite{talkmovesaaai}---pedagogical dialogue moves such as
\textit{Revoicing} and \textit{Pressing for Reasoning}---were operationalised
for NLP by fine-tuning ELECTRA on math classroom transcripts, and packaged
in Edu-ConvoKit~\cite{educonvokit}.  The closest prior work is the TIMSS
Video Study~\cite{timss1999video}, which relied entirely on manual coding;
our system automates and scales this analysis.

We introduce a framework that operationalises \emph{pedagogical friction}
and produces a post-session \emph{Teacher Friction Report}.  Contributions:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item An end-to-end multimodal pipeline (DeepFace + Whisper + LLM)
        with a three-tier strategy annotator hierarchy and graceful fallback.
  \item Large-scale transcript analysis across 53 TIMSS lessons spanning
        7 countries, exposing a cross-annotator correlation of $r{=}{-}0.35$.
  \item Human-annotated ground truth for AU1 and the first evidence that
        LLM reranking raises friction-detection F1 from 0.10 to 1.00.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

Figure~\ref{fig:model} shows the system overview.

\textbf{Vision stream.}
Frames are sampled at $r{=}2$\,Hz.
DeepFace~\cite{deepface} estimates seven per-frame emotion probabilities,
aggregated to a scalar confusion proxy $c_t\!\in\![0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
  c_t = \sum_{e} w_e \cdot p_t(e),
  \label{eq:conf}
\end{equation}
where the per-emotion weights are fixed at
$w_{\text{fear}}{=}0.90$,
$w_{\text{sad}}{=}0.85$,
$w_{\text{disgust}}{=}0.50$,
$w_{\text{angry}}{=}0.45$,
$w_{\text{surprise}}{=}0.25$, and
$w_{\text{neutral}}{=}w_{\text{happy}}{=}0$.
A trailing 30-second mean yields the smoothed timeline $C(t)$.

\textbf{Language stream.}
Faster-Whisper~\cite{whisper} transcribes audio; for transcript-only runs,
TIMSS \texttt{.txt} files are parsed directly.  A three-tier annotator
selects at runtime: (1)~\textbf{ELECTRA} (\texttt{YaHi/teacher\_electra\_small},
7-class Talk Moves~\cite{talkmovesaaai}); (2)~Edu-ConvoKit~\cite{educonvokit};
(3)~keyword heuristic.  ELECTRA labels map to quality:
\textit{Keeping Everyone Together}, \textit{Revoicing}, and
\textit{Pressing for Reasoning} are high-quality; \textit{Pressing for
Accuracy} is high-pressure; \textit{No Move} is neutral.

\textbf{Temporal alignment and $z$-score filter.}
Sessions are partitioned into $G{=}30$-second bins.  A bin is flagged
as a \emph{friction candidate} when
\begin{equation}
  \bar{c}_k > \mu_C + z\,\sigma_C
  \quad\text{and}\quad q_k \neq \text{high},
  \label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_C$, $\sigma_C$ are session-level statistics and $z$ is a
sensitivity parameter (default $z{=}1.0$).
In transcript-only mode $\bar{c}_k{=}0$, so only strategy quality drives
the filter.

\textbf{LLM fusion.}
Each candidate is sent to Claude (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}) with the
confusion score, strategy label, and transcript excerpt.  The model
returns structured JSON: \texttt{friction}\,(bool),
\texttt{rationale}\,(str), \texttt{alternative\_strategy}\,(str).
Frames are base64-encoded when available; the prompt is text-only
in transcript-only mode.

\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Qualitative Examples}

label: the teacher states \textit{``it will be 180 degrees''}, bypassing

\subsection{Full Multimodal Pipeline (AU1 Video)}

\textbf{Setup.}
TIMSS AU1 \emph{Exterior Angles}, first 10\,min.
DeepFace processes 1\,200 frames ($r{=}2$\,Hz); Whisper (\texttt{base},
int8) transcribes audio; heuristic labels strategy; Claude reranks.

\textbf{Results.}
The heuristic pre-filter flags 7 candidates (Figure~\ref{fig:confusion}).
The highest confusion score ($\bar{c}{=}0.814$, $z{=}2.28$) occurs at
1:30--2:00 when late-arriving students enter the room.  LLM fusion
rejects all 7: the elevated affect reflects \emph{social disruption},
not pedagogical confusion.  This reveals a key failure mode of
vision-only pipelines---off-task affective events produce high confusion
scores that transcript-level reasoning is needed to filter out.

\subsection{Scale-Up: 53 TIMSS Lessons (Transcript-Only)}

\textbf{Setup.}
All available TIMSS transcripts: 28 math + 25 science lessons, 7 countries
(AU, CZ, HK, JP, NL, SW, US), 5\,113 thirty-second bins total.
Both the heuristic (\texttt{flag\_unknown=True}) and ELECTRA
(\texttt{flag\_unknown=False}) annotators were run on the same corpus.

\textbf{Results.}
The heuristic flags 1\,611 bins (31.5\%); ELECTRA flags 988 (18.9\%).
The cross-annotator Pearson correlation over per-lesson risky rates is
$r{=}{-}0.35$ (Figure~\ref{fig:electra_heuristic}), indicating substantial
disagreement.  US math lessons show the sharpest divergence: heuristic
42.9\%, ELECTRA 14.5\% (Figure~\ref{fig:risky_country}), suggesting the
keyword heuristic over-fires on American rhetorical discourse style.

\subsection{Human Evaluation and LLM Fusion (AU1 Full Lesson)}

Human annotation of all 87 AU1 bins finds only \textbf{1 genuine friction
moment} (1.1\%): bin 11 (5:00--5:30), where the teacher directly states
the answer students should discover.  Table~\ref{table:results} compares
all conditions.  The heuristic achieves P=0.05 / R=1.00 / F1=0.10---it
captures the true positive but generates 19 false alarms.  ELECTRA
achieves F1=0.00, missing the true positive entirely because the utterance
maps to \textit{No Move} rather than a low-quality label.
Claude fusion over the 20 heuristic candidates yields
\textbf{P=1.00 / R=1.00 / F1=1.00}: it correctly rejects pre-class setup,
procedural software guidance, and vocabulary scaffolding, retaining only
the one genuine friction window.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

\textbf{Two-stage pipeline.}
The key finding is that heuristic detection and LLM reranking are
complementary: the heuristic provides high recall (it finds the only true
friction moment) while Claude provides the precision needed to eliminate
false alarms from pre-class setup, software guidance, and vocabulary
scaffolding.  Neither stage alone is sufficient.

\textbf{Social vs.\ cognitive confusion.}
The full video pipeline reveals a second failure mode: facial affect peaks
during social disruptions (late arrivals, seating issues) rather than
instructional confusion.  Vision-only systems cannot distinguish these;
transcript-level reasoning is essential.

\textbf{Annotator disagreement and cultural context.}
The $r{=}{-}0.35$ cross-annotator correlation and US anomaly (heuristic
42.9\% vs.\ ELECTRA 14.5\%) demonstrate that annotation methodology is
itself a critical design variable.  Keyword heuristics fire on surface
patterns; discourse-move classifiers label structural properties; neither
aligns well with human judgment without a reranking stage.

\textbf{Limitations and future work.}
We note that F1=1.00 is computed over a single positive sample (bin~11 of
AU1), which gives a wide Wilson 95\% confidence interval
$[0.21, 1.00]$; the result demonstrates end-to-end feasibility of the
two-stage pipeline rather than statistical significance, and broader
validation is needed.  Human annotation covers one lesson by one
annotator.  The confusion proxy uses fixed weights not validated against
ground-truth affect labels.  Future work should collect multi-annotator
labels across diverse lesson types and integrate full-video fusion via
GPU-based VLMs.

In summary, we presented an end-to-end pedagogical friction detector,
scaled transcript analysis to 53 TIMSS lessons across 7 countries, and
showed that a two-stage heuristic + LLM pipeline achieves human-level
precision (F1=1.00) on the annotated AU1 lesson.  Code and data are
available at \url{https://github.com/jw310-wjr/comp646-friction-detector}.
