\title{Multimodal Pedagogical Friction Detector:\\
Fusing Facial Affect, Talk Moves, and LLM Reasoning\\
to Surface Teachable Moments at Scale}

\author{Jingrui Wu\\
Department of Computer Science, Rice University\\
{\tt\small jw310@rice.edu}
}

\maketitle

%%%%%%%%% ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
We present an automated system for detecting \emph{pedagogical friction}:
moments where a low-quality instructional strategy co-occurs with elevated
student confusion.  Our pipeline fuses per-frame facial affect
(DeepFace~\cite{deepface}), automatic speech recognition
(Whisper~\cite{whisper}), and Talk Move classification
(ELECTRA~\cite{talkmovesaaai}) on a 30-second temporal grid, then
applies \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} as a second-pass precision filter
that supplies both a friction verdict and an actionable alternative
strategy.  Evaluated on a multimodal run on 10\,min of TIMSS video,
transcript-only analysis of \textbf{53 lessons} across 7 countries
(5\,113 bins), and human annotation on AU1, the pipeline
achieves \textbf{F1\,=\,1.00} on that lesson (vs.\ F1\,=\,0.10 for
the heuristic), reduces transcript-only candidates $7.6\times$ to
211 friction moments, and reveals a cross-method correlation of
$r{=}{-}0.35$, marking annotation methodology
as a first-class design variable.
\end{abstract}

%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

High-quality teaching requires \emph{professional vision}, the ability
to notice and interpret significant events in classroom
interaction~\cite{sherinvanes2009}.  When student understanding
stalls, the teacher's next move is critical: giving the answer
directly, pressing for rote recall, or ignoring the confusion all
prevent the productive cognitive engagement necessary for deep
learning~\cite{dmello2012}.  We call such moments
\emph{pedagogical friction}: the co-occurrence of elevated student
confusion and a low-quality instructional response.

Existing automated tools address only one modality at a time.
Edu-ConvoKit~\cite{educonvokit} and Tutor~CoPilot~\cite{tutorcopilot}
analyse transcript structure but ignore student affect;
facial-affect systems detect confusion but cannot interpret whether
the teacher's response was appropriate.  The TIMSS 1999 Video
Study~\cite{timss1999video} manually coded thousands of clips across
seven countries, a gold standard requiring years of expert effort
that cannot scale to real-time feedback.

We bridge this gap with a system that fuses vision and language
signals on a shared 30-second temporal grid, applies
\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} as a multimodal reranker generating
concrete alternative strategies, and scales to 53 transcript lessons
plus 54 TIMSS videos.  On AU1, the two-stage design raises
friction-detection F1 from 0.10 to 1.00; across the transcript
corpus, it reduces candidate load by $7.6\times$.
Code is available at \url{https://github.com/jw310-wjr/comp646-friction-detector}.

\section{Related Work}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Suresh et al.~\cite{talkmovesaaai} fine-tune ELECTRA on Talk Move
classification; Wang and Demszky~\cite{educonvokit} and Wang et
al.~\cite{tutorcopilot} provide NLP utilities and an LLM copilot for
educational dialogues---neither fuses discourse quality with student affect.
D'Mello and Graesser~\cite{dmello2012} show confusion and frustration are
most predictive of learning disruption, motivating Eq.~\ref{eq:conf}.
Hiebert et al.~\cite{timss1999video} and Sherin and van
Es~\cite{sherinvanes2009} demonstrate the value of systematic video coding
and professional vision training, which our pipeline automates at scale.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the two-stream architecture.
A \textbf{Vision Stream} (blue) and a \textbf{Language Stream} (purple)
run in parallel on a shared 30-second temporal grid, converge at a
\emph{z}-score filter, and produce a structured friction verdict via
\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} fusion.  Figure~\ref{fig:model} shows a
concrete input/output example for the highest-confidence friction
moment in our dataset.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pipeline_overview.pdf}
\caption{Two-stream pipeline: Vision (blue, Eq.\,\ref{eq:conf}) and Language (purple) streams share a 30-s grid; a $z$-score filter (Eq.\,\ref{eq:filter}) selects candidates for \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} friction verdict and alternative strategy.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Vision Stream: Confusion Timeline}

Video is sampled at $r{=}2$\,Hz.  Each frame is passed to
DeepFace~\cite{deepface}, which returns a probability vector
$\mathbf{p}_t\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^7$ over Ekman's seven basic emotions.
We map this to a scalar \emph{confusion proxy} $c_t\!\in\![0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
  c_t = \sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}} w_e \cdot p_t(e),
  \label{eq:conf}
\end{equation}
where weights are derived from the affect-outcome correlations in
D'Mello and Graesser~\cite{dmello2012}:
$w_{\text{fear}}{=}0.90$, $w_{\text{sad}}{=}0.85$,
$w_{\text{disgust}}{=}0.50$, $w_{\text{angry}}{=}0.45$,
$w_{\text{surprise}}{=}0.25$, $w_{\text{happy}}{=}w_{\text{neutral}}{=}0$
(higher weight = stronger empirical link to learning disruption).
A trailing 30-second mean yields smoothed $C(t)$.

\subsection{Language Stream: Talk Move Classification}

Audio is transcribed with Faster-Whisper~\cite{whisper}
(\texttt{small}, CUDA float16); plain-text TIMSS transcripts are
parsed directly when available.  The Talk Move annotator follows a
three-tier hierarchy: (1)~\textbf{ELECTRA}
(\texttt{YaHi/teacher\_electra\_small}) fine-tuned on the TalkMoves
corpus~\cite{talkmovesaaai}; (2)~\textbf{Edu-ConvoKit}~\cite{educonvokit}
as fallback; (3)~\textbf{keyword heuristic} as final fallback.
ELECTRA labels map to quality signal $q$: \textit{Keeping Everyone
Together}, \textit{Revoicing}, \textit{Pressing for Reasoning}
$\to$ \emph{high}; \textit{Pressing for Accuracy} $\to$ \emph{high-pressure};
\textit{No Move}, \textit{Other} $\to$ \emph{unknown}.

\subsection{Grid Alignment, $z$-Score Filter, and LLM Fusion}

For each 30-second bin $k$, $\bar{c}_k$ is the mean of $C(t)$ over
$[t_k, t_k{+}30)$.  A bin is a \emph{friction candidate} when
\begin{equation}
  \bar{c}_k > \mu_C + z\,\sigma_C
  \quad\text{and}\quad q_k \neq \textit{high},
  \label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_C, \sigma_C$ are session-level statistics and $z{=}1.0$
by default, flagging the top $\sim$16\% of bins.  When DeepFace detects no faces (all $\bar{c}_k{=}0$),
candidates are selected on strategy quality alone.  Each candidate
is then sent to \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} with the confusion score,
Talk Move label, a 1{,}200-character transcript excerpt, and up to three
video frames.  The model returns
{\small\ttfamily\{"friction": bool, "rationale": str,
"alternative\_strategy": str\}}.
All reported metrics use the \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} backend.

\section{Experiments and Results}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Multimodal Pipeline: AU1 Video and 54-Video Scale-Up}

TIMSS lesson AU1~\cite{timssvideo} (\textit{Exterior Angles},
Australia) was processed end-to-end: 1\,200 frames through DeepFace,
audio through Whisper, heuristic strategy labels, and
\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} reranking.
Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} shows the full bin-level quality timeline.
The highest confusion peak ($\bar{c}{=}0.814$, $z{=}2.28$) falls at
1:30--2:00 when late-arriving students enter; the model correctly
rejects all 7 candidates, identifying the peak as a social disruption
rather than instructional friction.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/strategy_timeline_mau1.pdf}
\caption{Strategy quality timeline for M-AU1 (87 bins). Green/red/grey = high/low/unknown quality; shaded = heuristic candidates; red triangle (bin\,11) = Claude-confirmed friction, matching the human-annotation positive.}
\label{fig:timeline}
\end{figure}

Scaling to all 54 TIMSS videos via SLURM GPU array jobs (NVIDIA A40),
DeepFace returned $\bar{c}_k{=}0$ for every bin (5\,318 bins total):
wide-angle cameras render student faces at $<$20 pixels, labelled
\textit{neutral} or \textit{happy}, both carrying zero weight in
Eq.~\ref{eq:conf}.  The pipeline fell back to strategy-only selection;
of 1\,797 flagged bins, LLM fusion confirmed \textbf{21 friction
moments} (0.4\% of bins).  This establishes a practical boundary:
DeepFace is unreliable for standard wide-angle classroom recordings.

\subsection{Scale-Up: 53 TIMSS Lessons (Transcript-Only)}

All 53 TIMSS lessons with available transcripts (28 math, 25 science;
7 countries; 5\,113 bins) were annotated by both the keyword heuristic
and ELECTRA.  The heuristic flags 1\,611 bins (31.5\%); ELECTRA flags
988 (19.3\%).  Figure~\ref{fig:country} shows that the US exhibits
the highest heuristic rate (43.7\%) yet one of the lowest ELECTRA rates
(11.2\%), a 32-percentage-point gap.  The Pearson correlation between
per-lesson risky rates is $r{=}{-}0.35$, indicating that the two labeling methods
frequently disagree on which lessons are most risky.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/risky_by_country.pdf}
\caption{Fraction of 30-s bins flagged as heuristically risky by
country and subject.  US peaks at 43.7\% (math); Japan is
systematically low ($r{=}{-}0.35$ vs.\ ELECTRA, $n{=}53$).}
\label{fig:country}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Human Evaluation and LLM Reranking (AU1)}

All 87 bins of AU1 were labelled by one trained annotator.  One
genuine friction moment was found (1.1\%): bin 11 (5:00--5:30),
where the teacher states \textit{``it will be 180 degrees''} after
students conjecture about exterior angle sums, short-circuiting the
productive struggle that would consolidate the concept.
Table~\ref{table:results} compares all conditions.  The keyword
heuristic achieves P\,=\,0.05 / R\,=\,1.00 / F1\,=\,0.10; ELECTRA
scores F1\,=\,0.00 because the critical utterance maps to
\textit{No Move}, not a low-quality label.  Claude fusion over the
20 heuristic candidates yields \textbf{F1\,=\,1.00}.

\subsection{Cross-Lesson Claude Fusion (53 Lessons)}

\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} confirmed \textbf{211 bins} as genuine
friction (13.1\% of 1\,611 candidates; $7.6\times$ reduction).
Math showed a higher confirmation rate (16.2\%) than science (9.2\%).
Figure~\ref{fig:heuristic_claude} disaggregates by country: Czech
Republic and Hong Kong reach $\sim$20\%, while Japan and the US fall
below 10\%.  Japan is over-flagged because culturally appropriate
direct instruction matches English-centric keywords; the US because
high heuristic recall captures rhetorical patterns that the model
does not judge as friction.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis}

Table~\ref{table:examples} presents six representative
\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}-confirmed friction windows across five countries.
Three anti-patterns recur: \textbf{answer-giving} (rows 1, 4) forecloses
productive struggle; \textbf{closed-question loops} (rows 2--3) accumulate
surface answers; \textbf{transmission-mode instruction} (row 6) bypasses
conceptual activation.  The model correctly rejects pre-class logistics
and vocabulary scaffolding, reading semantic content rather than keywords.

\section{Discussion}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\textbf{Two-stage design and cultural variability.}
The keyword heuristic achieves R\,=\,1.00 but P\,=\,0.05, firing on
logistics and scaffolding.  ELECTRA misses semantic friction because
answer-giving maps to \emph{No Move}.  \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}
reads meaning rather than surface structure, complementing both.
The $r{=}{-}0.35$ cross-method correlation and 32\,pp US gap reveal annotation
methodology as a first-class design variable; Japanese lessons are
over-flagged due to English-centric keywords (Fig.~\ref{fig:heuristic_claude}).

\textbf{Limitations.}
The F1\,=\,1.00 result is a proof of concept: one lesson, one annotator,
one positive example.  Credible generalisation requires multi-lesson
evaluation with inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's $\kappa$).
Emotion weights (Eq.~\ref{eq:conf}) use published correlations rather than
classroom-optimised values; wide-angle cameras make vision a weak signal.
All results use \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} at temperature\,=\,0.

%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
%─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We presented a multimodal pedagogical friction detector fusing facial
affect, Talk Move classification, and LLM reranking.  On AU1, the
system achieves F1\,=\,1.00; across 53 transcript lessons it reduces
heuristic candidates by $7.6\times$.  Across 54 TIMSS videos,
wide-angle cameras make vision unreliable, establishing the language
stream as the primary signal.  The $r{=}{-}0.35$ cross-method
correlation confirms that labeling methodology is a first-class
design variable in classroom NLP.
