%%%%%%%%% ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
Effective teaching depends on noticing student confusion and adjusting
instruction.  We present a system that detects \emph{pedagogical friction}:
moments where a low-quality instructional strategy coincides with visible
student confusion.  Our pipeline constructs a confusion timeline from
per-frame facial affect (DeepFace~\cite{deepface}), a strategy timeline
from transcribed speech (Whisper~\cite{whisper}) annotated by either a
heuristic rule set or an ELECTRA Talk Moves classifier~\cite{talkmovesaaai},
temporally aligns both signals on a 30-second grid, and flags candidate
windows via a statistical $z$-score filter.  Flagged windows are passed to
Qwen2.5-VL~\cite{qwen25vl} for friction confirmation and
alternative-strategy generation.  We evaluate at two scales: a CPU smoke
test on the TIMSS AU1 \emph{Exterior Angles} clip (5\,min, 3 friction
candidates) and a transcript-only analysis across \textbf{53 TIMSS lessons}
spanning 7 countries and both math and science subjects.  The heuristic
annotator flags 31.5\% of 30-second bins as risky; the ELECTRA annotator
flags 18.9\%, with a negative cross-annotator correlation ($r{=}{-}0.35$),
motivating human-label evaluation as the next milestone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

High-quality teaching requires moment-by-moment diagnosis of student
understanding and rapid strategy adjustment~\cite{sherinvanes2009}.
Tools such as Tutor CoPilot~\cite{tutorcopilot} and
Edu-ConvoKit~\cite{educonvokit} surface useful feedback from transcripts,
and transformer models can detect productive talk
moves~\cite{talkmovesaaai}, yet no pipeline jointly models strategy quality
and student affect to surface actionable friction moments.
Confusion has been identified as a pivotal affective state that, when
unaddressed, impedes deep learning~\cite{dmello2012}.
Large vision-language models such as Qwen2.5-VL~\cite{qwen25vl} now make
it feasible to synthesise video frames, transcripts, and affect signals in
a single inference pass.

We introduce a framework that operationalises \emph{pedagogical
friction}---the joint occurrence of a risky instructional strategy and
elevated student confusion---and produces a post-session
\emph{Teacher Friction Report} with timestamps and suggested instructional
moves.  Our contributions are: (i)~a working end-to-end multimodal
pipeline; (ii)~a public codebase at
\texttt{github.com/jw310-wjr/comp646-friction-detector};
(iii)~a scale-up transcript analysis across 53 TIMSS lessons~\cite{timss1999video};
and (iv)~a comparison of heuristic vs.\ ELECTRA strategy annotators.

\section{Methodology}

Figure~\ref{fig:model} shows a schematic overview.

\textbf{Vision stream.}
Frames are sampled at $r{=}2$\,Hz.
DeepFace~\cite{deepface} estimates per-frame emotion probabilities, which
are mapped to a scalar confusion proxy $c_t\!\in\![0,1]$ via a fixed
weight table: neutral/happy\,$\to\!0$; fear/sad\,$\to\!0.85$--$0.9$;
disgust/angry\,$\to\!0.45$--$0.5$.
A trailing 30-second mean yields the confusion timeline $C(t)$.

\textbf{Language stream.}
faster-Whisper~\cite{whisper} transcribes audio with VAD filtering.
A three-tier \emph{strategy annotator} hierarchy is used: (1)~an ELECTRA
Talk Moves classifier (\texttt{YaHi/teacher\_electra\_small},
7-class~\cite{talkmovesaaai}) loaded directly via HuggingFace
\texttt{transformers}; (2)~the Edu-ConvoKit wrapper~\cite{educonvokit} as
fallback; (3)~a keyword heuristic as offline fallback.
The ELECTRA labels map to quality: high-quality moves include
\textit{Keeping Everyone Together}, \textit{Revoicing}, and
\textit{Pressing for Reasoning}; \textit{Pressing for Accuracy} is treated
as high-pressure (risky).
For transcript-only runs, TIMSS \texttt{.txt} files are parsed directly,
avoiding the need for video or ASR.

\textbf{Temporal alignment and filtering.}
Sessions are partitioned into $G{=}30$-second bins.
Per-bin mean confusion $\bar{c}_k$ and strategy quality label
$q_k\!\in\!\{\text{high},\text{low},\text{unknown}\}$ are computed.
A bin is flagged as a \emph{friction candidate} when
\begin{equation}
  \bar{c}_k > \mu_C + z\,\sigma_C
  \quad\text{and}\quad q_k \neq \text{high},
  \label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_C$, $\sigma_C$ are the session-level mean and s.d.\ of confusion
scores, and $z$ is a sensitivity parameter.
In transcript-only mode, $\bar{c}_k{=}0$ for all bins, so only
strategy quality drives the filter (Eq.~\ref{eq:filter} reduces to
$q_k \neq \text{high}$).

\textbf{VLM fusion (optional).}
For each candidate, Qwen2.5-VL~\cite{qwen25vl} receives 1--4 frames,
a confusion summary, strategy label, and transcript excerpt, returning
structured JSON: \texttt{friction}\,(bool), \texttt{rationale}\,(str),
\texttt{alternative\_strategy}\,(str).

\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{CPU Smoke Test (AU1 Clip)}

\textbf{Setup.}
TIMSS AU1 \emph{Exterior Angles} (geometry, Australian Grade~8), 5-minute
clip.
CPU path: Python~3.9, OpenCV, DeepFace (TF~2.x), faster-Whisper
(\texttt{base}, int8).
Key hyperparameters: $r{=}2$\,Hz, $G{=}30$\,s.

\textbf{Results.}
With $z{=}1.0$ and the heuristic annotator, the AU1 teacher's
question-based discourse (\textit{``What did you learn?''}, \textit{``Do
you agree?''}) falls outside the keyword vocabulary, giving
$q_k{=}\text{high}$ for all bins and zero candidates.
Lowering to $z{=}0.3$ with \texttt{flag\_unknown\_strategy} produces
\textbf{3 candidates} at 150--240\,s where per-bin confusion peaks at
0.55--0.70 (Table~\ref{table:results}).

\subsection{Scale-Up: 53 TIMSS Lessons (Transcript-Only)}

\textbf{Setup.}
We parsed the human-authored TIMSS transcript \texttt{.txt} files for all
available lessons: 28 math and 25 science lessons spanning 7 countries
(AU, CZ, HK, JP, NL, SW, US), totalling 5\,113 30-second bins.
Transcript-only mode sets $\bar{c}_k{=}0$, so only strategy quality flags
bins.  We ran two annotators:
(a)~\textbf{Heuristic} (keyword rules, \texttt{flag\_unknown=True}); and
(b)~\textbf{ELECTRA} (\texttt{YaHi/teacher\_electra\_small},
\texttt{flag\_unknown=False}).

\textbf{Results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:risky_country} shows per-country risky-bin rates for both
annotators.  The heuristic flags 31.5\% of bins overall (1\,611/5\,113),
while ELECTRA flags 18.9\% (988/5\,113).  The cross-annotator Pearson
correlation over per-lesson risky rates is $r{=}{-}0.35$, indicating that
the two methods disagree substantially on which lessons have the most risky
bins.  This motivates collecting human annotations to adjudicate.

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} breaks down strategy-quality distribution by
lesson.  US math lessons show the highest heuristic friction rate (42.9\%),
while ELECTRA rates in the US are among the lowest (14.5\%), suggesting
the keyword heuristic over-fires on American discourse style.
Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} shows the per-bin strategy quality timeline for
AU1, the representative lesson used in the smoke test.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented an end-to-end multimodal pedagogical friction detector and
scaled the transcript-only pipeline to 53 TIMSS lessons across 7 countries.
The heuristic annotator flags 31.5\% of 30-second bins as risky on average,
while the ELECTRA Talk Moves classifier flags only 18.9\%, with near-zero
positive correlation ($r{=}{-}0.35$) between the two.  This divergence
reveals that annotation methodology is a critical variable, and ground-truth
human labels are essential before reporting meaningful precision or recall.
Next steps: (1)~run Qwen2.5-VL on AU1 friction candidates on the Rice GPU
cluster; (2)~collect dual human annotations on 1--2 full lessons and compute
Precision/Recall/F1 for both annotators; (3)~investigate why heuristic
rates are highest in the US and ELECTRA rates are lowest there.
Automated facial affect inference may misread culture- or
disability-related cues; all pipeline outputs are advisory and require
human review.
